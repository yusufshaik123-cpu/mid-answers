Series Circuit Analysis

Parallel Circuit Analysis
For resistors R₁, R₂, R₃ connected in PARALLEL: 1/R_eq = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ Example: R₁ = 10Ω, R₂ = 20Ω, R₃ = 30Ω, V = 60V 1/R_eq = 1/10 + 1/20 + 1/30 = 6/60 + 3/60 + 2/60 = 11/60 R_eq = 60/11 ≈ 5.45Ω I_total = V / R_eq = 60 / 5.45 ≈ 11 A Branch currents: I₁ = V/R₁ = 60/10 = 6A I₂ = V/R₂ = 60/20 = 3A I₃ = V/R₃ = 60/30 = 2A I_total = 6 + 3 + 2 = 11A ✓
Part (b): Power Factor and its Significance
Definition of Power Factor

Power Factor (PF) is the ratio of Active (Real) Power (P) consumed by a circuit to the Apparent Power (S) supplied to the circuit.

Power Factor = P / S = cos φ
Where: P = Active Power (Watts, W) — useful work done S = Apparent Power (Volt-Amperes, VA) — total power supplied Q = Reactive Power (VAR) — stored/returned power Power Factor = cos φ (dimensionless, ranges from 0 to 1) φ = phase angle between voltage and current
Power Factor Triangle
S (Apparent Power, VA) │╲ │ ╲ Q │ ╲ S = √(P² + Q²) │ ╲ └────────╲ P (Real Power, W) cos φ = P/S (Power Factor) sin φ = Q/S tan φ = Q/P
Significance of Power Factor
  • Unity PF (cos φ = 1): Pure resistive load — all power is consumed as useful work (ideal condition).
  • Zero PF (cos φ = 0): Pure reactive load — no real power consumed, all power is reactive.
  • Low PF (0 to 0.8): More current drawn for the same real power → increased I²R losses in transmission lines.
  • Economic Significance: Electricity boards penalize industries with low PF (below 0.85). Higher PF reduces electricity bills.
  • Equipment Rating: Generators, transformers, cables are rated in kVA. Low PF means less kW output for same kVA capacity.
  • Power Factor Correction: Achieved by connecting capacitor banks in parallel for inductive loads (motors, transformers).
✅
R_eq(series) = ΣR; R_eq(parallel) = 1/Σ(1/R); I = V/R_eq. Power Factor = cos φ = P/S; Low PF increases losses and electricity cost; corrected using capacitors.

Part (a) & (b): Kirchhoff's Laws
Fig: KCL – Sum of currents at a node
Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) — Definition

Statement: The algebraic sum of all currents entering and leaving a junction (node) is zero.

ΣI_entering = ΣI_leaving     OR     ΣI = 0 at any node

Based on the Law of Conservation of Charge — charge cannot accumulate at a node.

Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) — Definition

Statement: The algebraic sum of all voltages (EMFs and voltage drops) around any closed loop is zero.

ΣV = 0    (around any closed loop)

Based on the Law of Conservation of Energy — energy gained from EMF sources equals energy lost in resistors.

Numerical — Applying KVL (Single Loop)
Circuit: Battery E = 20V, R₁ = 4Ω, R₂ = 6Ω in series Applying KVL (clockwise loop): +E − I×R₁ − I×R₂ = 0 20 − 4I − 6I = 0 20 = 10I I = 2 A Voltage drops: V_R1 = I × R₁ = 2 × 4 = 8V V_R2 = I × R₂ = 2 × 6 = 12V Verification: 8 + 12 = 20V = E ✓ (KVL verified)
Numerical — Applying KCL (Junction with 3 branches)
At node A, three branches meet: I₁ = 6A (entering) I₂ = 4A (entering) I₃ = ? (leaving) Applying KCL: ΣI_entering = ΣI_leaving I₁ + I₂ = I₃ 6 + 4 = I₃ I₃ = 10 A (leaving the node) Check: ΣI = 6 + 4 − 10 = 0 ✓ (KCL verified)
✅
KCL: ΣI_in = ΣI_out at any node. KVL: ΣV = 0 around any closed loop. Both verified numerically.

Part (a): Ohm's Law and its Limitations
Fig: Ohm's Law – Linear V-I Characteristic
Statement of Ohm's Law

Statement: At constant temperature, the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference (voltage) applied across it.

V = I × R     OR     I = V/R     OR     R = V/I
Where: V = Voltage (Volts, V) I = Current (Amperes, A) R = Resistance (Ohms, Ω) — constant of proportionality Example: V = 12V, R = 4Ω → I = 12/4 = 3A
Limitations of Ohm's Law
  • Temperature Dependence: Valid only at constant temperature. Resistance of metals increases with temperature.
  • Non-linear Devices: Does not apply to diodes, transistors, thermistors, and LDRs where V-I is not linear.
  • Electrolytes: Does not apply to electrolytic solutions — current causes chemical changes that alter resistance.
  • Arc & gas discharge tubes: Current varies non-linearly with voltage.
  • Semiconductors: Silicon, germanium do not obey Ohm's law strictly.
  • Magnetic materials: Resistance changes due to magnetic effects break the linear relationship.

Part (b): AC Terminologies
Fig: AC sinusoidal waveform showing key parameters
(i) Time Period (T)

Definition: The time taken for one complete cycle of an alternating quantity.

T = 1/f     (seconds)

For Indian supply (50 Hz): T = 1/50 = 0.02 seconds = 20 ms

(ii) Frequency (f)

Definition: The number of complete cycles completed per second by an alternating quantity.

f = 1/T     (Hertz, Hz)

India: 50 Hz; USA: 60 Hz. Higher frequency → faster oscillation.

(iii) Amplitude (Peak Value, Vm)

Definition: The maximum value reached by the alternating quantity in either the positive or negative half-cycle.

v(t) = V_m sin(ωt)  →  Amplitude = V_m

For 230V RMS supply: V_m = 230 × √2 ≈ 325.27V

(iv) Peak-to-Peak Value (Vpp)

Definition: The total voltage variation from maximum positive peak to maximum negative peak.

V_pp = 2 × V_m

For 230V RMS supply: V_pp = 2 × 325.27 ≈ 650.54V

Part (b): Derivation of RMS and Average Values
RMS Value — Derivation

RMS: Equivalent DC value that produces the same heating effect as the AC in a resistive circuit.

For v(t) = V_m sin(ωt), over one full period T: V_rms = √[ (1/T) ∫₀ᵀ v²(t) dt ] Step 1: v²(t) = V_m² sin²(ωt) = V_m²(1 − cos 2ωt)/2 Step 2: (1/T) ∫₀ᵀ [V_m²/2 × (1 − cos 2ωt)] dt Step 3: = V_m²/2 × [T − 0]/T = V_m²/2 Step 4: V_rms = √(V_m²/2) = V_m/√2 ≈ 0.707 V_m ∴ V_rms = V_m / √2
Average Value — Derivation

Average Value: Mean of all instantaneous values over a half cycle.

For v(t) = V_m sin(ωt), over half period (0 to π/ω): V_avg = (ω/π) ∫₀^(π/ω) V_m sin(ωt) dt = (V_m/π) × [−cos(ωt)]₀^(π/ω) = (V_m/π) × [−cos(π) + cos(0)] = (V_m/π) × 2 ∴ V_avg = 2V_m / π ≈ 0.637 V_m
✅
V_m=10V, f=0.637Hz, T=1.571s, V_rms=7.071V, V_avg=6.366V, φ=+45°. Derivation: V_rms = V_m/√2 ≈ 0.707V_m; V_avg = 2V_m/π ≈ 0.637V_m.

Part (a): DC Generator
Key Principle: Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction — "When a conductor moves in a magnetic field, an EMF is induced in it."
EMF equation: E = ΦZNP/60A   where Φ=flux/pole, Z=conductors, N=RPM, P=poles, A=parallel paths
What is an Electrical Machine?
  • Generator: Converts mechanical energy → Electrical energy
  • Motor: Converts electrical energy → Mechanical energy
  • Transformer: Transfers electrical energy between circuits (changes voltage/current levels)
Construction of DC Generator
  • Stator (Field System): Poles with field windings produce magnetic flux. Yoke provides mechanical support.
  • Armature (Rotor): Rotating cylindrical laminated iron core carrying armature conductors in slots.
  • Commutator: Segmented copper cylinder — converts alternating induced EMF to unidirectional (DC) output.
  • Brushes: Carbon brushes maintain sliding contact with commutator to collect current.
Working Principle
1. Field windings carry DC → strong magnetic field (N and S poles). 2. Armature rotated by prime mover (engine/turbine). 3. Conductors cut magnetic flux → EMF induced (Faraday's Law: e = Blv sinθ) 4. Induced EMF is alternating (AC) in armature. 5. Commutator rectifies AC → DC at output terminals. 6. Carbon brushes collect DC and supply to external circuit. EMF equation: E = (ΦZNP) / (60A) Φ = flux per pole (Wb), Z = total armature conductors N = speed (rpm), P = number of poles A = parallel paths (= 2 for wave winding, = P for lap)
Part (b): Megger — High Resistance Insulation Tester
What is a Megger & How it Works

A Megger (Megaohmmeter) measures very high resistances — particularly insulation resistance of cables, motors, and transformers. It applies high DC voltage (500V, 1000V, or 2500V) and measures leakage current to give resistance in Megaohms (MΩ).

Components: 1. Hand-operated DC generator (or battery + DC-DC converter) 2. Two coils — Current Coil & Pressure/Voltage Coil (cross-coil movement) 3. Deflecting coil connected across measured resistance R_x 4. Control coil in series with fixed resistor R_s Working: • Torque by current coil ∝ V/R_x • Torque by pressure coil ∝ V • At balance → pointer indicates R_x in MΩ Scale Readings: ∞ MΩ → Perfect insulation >1 MΩ → Acceptable insulation <0.5 MΩ → Insulation needs replacement 0 MΩ → Dead short (complete breakdown)
✅
DC Generator: Faraday's law → rotating armature → EMF = ΦZNP/60A → commutator converts AC to DC. Megger: applies high DC voltage, measures leakage current → insulation resistance in MΩ.

Part (b): Single Phase Induction Motor — Not Self-Starting
Why Not Self-Starting?
Single-phase supply produces a PULSATING (not rotating) magnetic field. Pulsating field = two equal, opposite rotating fields: F = F_forward + F_backward At standstill (N = 0): T_forward = T_backward → Net torque = 0 → Motor cannot start. Once given a push in either direction: That direction's torque dominates → motor accelerates. Solution: Create artificial phase difference to produce starting torque.
Type 1 — Split-Phase Induction Motor
Main winding: Low R, high L (high inductance) Auxiliary winding: High R, low L • I_main and I_aux are out of phase by ~30° → starting torque. • Centrifugal switch disconnects auxiliary at ~75% full speed. Applications: Fans, blowers, washing machines.
Type 2 — Capacitor-Start Induction Motor
Auxiliary winding in series with CAPACITOR. • Capacitor causes I_aux to LEAD by ~90°. • Phase difference ≈ 90° → better rotating field → higher starting torque. • Centrifugal switch disconnects auxiliary at 75% speed. Starting torque = 3–4 × full load torque (much higher than split-phase). Applications: Compressors, pumps, refrigerators, air conditioners.
✅
Back EMF = V − I_aR_a; regulates motor speed. 1-phase IM not self-starting — zero net torque at standstill. Split-phase and capacitor-start motors create artificial phase difference for starting torque.

Part (a): Role of Commutator in DC Machines
Role in DC Generator

Acts as a mechanical rectifier — commutator segments reverse their brush connections every half revolution, ensuring current always flows in one direction in the external circuit → unidirectional (DC) output.

Role in DC Motor

Acts as a mechanical inverter — reverses the direction of current in armature conductors every half rotation, ensuring torque always acts in the same direction for continuous rotation. Without it, the motor would only oscillate.

Part (b): Wheatstone Bridge — Medium Resistance Measurement
Fig: Wheatstone Bridge – balanced condition gives unknown resistance
Derivation of Balance Condition
Circuit: P, Q (ratio arms), R (variable known), X (unknown), G (galvanometer) At BALANCE: I_G = 0 → V_B = V_D From P-R branch: V_B = E × R / (P + R) From Q-X branch: V_D = E × X / (Q + X) Setting equal: R / (P+R) = X / (Q+X) R(Q+X) = X(P+R) RQ + RX = XP + XR RQ = XP ∴ X = (P/Q) × R ← BALANCE CONDITION Procedure: 1. Connect unknown X in one arm. 2. Choose ratio P/Q (e.g., 1:1, 1:10, 10:1). 3. Vary R until galvanometer reads ZERO. 4. At balance: X = (P/Q) × R Range: 1Ω to 100kΩ (medium resistance), accuracy < 0.1%
✅
Commutator: Generator → rectifies AC to DC; Motor → reverses current for continuous torque. Wheatstone Bridge: at balance X = (P/Q)×R. Used for medium resistance (1Ω–100kΩ).

Part (a): PMMC Instrument
Construction
  • Permanent Magnet: U-shaped, provides strong radial magnetic field in air gap.
  • Moving Coil: Rectangular aluminum former wound with fine copper wire, pivoted on jewel bearings.
  • Cylindrical Iron Core: Concentrates and makes field uniform and radial.
  • Control Springs: Phosphor-bronze hair springs provide controlling torque; also serve as current paths.
  • Pointer & Scale: Light aluminum pointer on calibrated linear scale.
  • Damping: Eddy currents in aluminum former oppose oscillation.
Working Principle
Deflecting Torque: T_d = N × B × A × I (T_d ∝ I) Controlling Torque: T_c = k × θ At equilibrium (T_d = T_c): θ = (NBA/k) × I → θ ∝ I ∴ Scale is LINEAR — deflection proportional to current. For DC: Pointer deflects correctly. For AC: Average torque = 0 (pointer doesn't deflect). ∴ PMMC works for DC ONLY.

Current I → coil magnetic field → iron vane(s) magnetized. T_d ∝ I² (Non-linear / square-law scale) For AC: T_d ∝ I_rms² → instrument reads RMS directly. For DC: T_d ∝ I_dc² → also works. At balance: C × I² = k × θ → θ = (C/k) × I² Damping: Air damping (aluminum vane in chamber)

PMMC: T_d = NBAI, θ ∝ I, linear scale, DC only. MI: T_d ∝ I², non-linear scale, works for AC & DC (reads RMS). Repulsion type more accurate than attraction type.

Principle: Chemical energy (coal) → Heat energy → Mechanical energy (turbine) → Electrical energy (generator).

Part (b): Electric Shock — Safety Precautions
What is Electric Shock?
Physiological reaction when electric current passes through the human body. Effects of 50Hz AC: 1 mA : Slight tingling (threshold) 5 mA : Painful, can let go 10–20 mA : Muscular contractions ("can't let go") 50–100 mA: Ventricular fibrillation — potentially fatal >200 mA : Severe burns, charring, death
Safety Precautions
  • Earthing/Grounding: All metallic parts earthed — leakage flows to earth, not through a person.
  • Fuses and MCBs: Disconnect supply when current exceeds safe limits.
  • ELCB / RCCB: Trips within 30ms when leakage exceeds 30mA — life-saving.
  • Insulation: All live conductors must have proper PVC/rubber insulation.
  • Isolation Before Work (LOTO): Switch off and lock out supply before working on equipment.
  • PPE: Rubber-soled boots, insulated gloves, safety helmets when working on electrical systems.
  • Dry Conditions: Never operate electrical equipment with wet hands or in damp surroundings.
  • Double Insulation: Class II portable appliances have no earthing pin needed.
  • First Aid: Switch off supply first — don't touch shocked person directly. Then provide CPR.
✅
Hydro PP: Reservoir→Penstock→Turbine→Generator. Electric shock: current through body; prevented by earthing, ELCB, insulation, PPE, dry conditions, isolation.

Part (b): Fuse and MCB
Fuse — Operation
Construction: Thin tin/lead alloy wire in ceramic/glass body. Working: Normal: I < rating → wire stays intact. Fault: I > rating → I²R heating → wire melts → circuit broken. Limitations: ✗ Single-use — must be replaced after operation. ✗ Slow response for small overcurrents. ✗ Cannot distinguish overload vs short circuit precisely.
MCB — Operation
Two protection elements: 1. BIMETALLIC STRIP (overload): Overcurrent → strip bends → trips. Operates in seconds to minutes (time-delay). 2. ELECTROMAGNETIC SOLENOID (short circuit): High fault current → strong magnetic pull → contacts open instantly (<10ms). Advantages over Fuse: ✓ Resettable — flip back ON after fault cleared. ✓ Faster response for short circuits. ✓ Can be used as a switch. ✓ Visual trip indication. Types by tripping curve: Type B: 3–5× rated current (resistive loads) Type C: 5–10× rated current (motors, fluorescent lights) Type D: 10–20× rated current (transformers, large motors)
✅
Wind plant: Blades→Gearbox→Generator→Power Electronics→Transformer→Grid. Fuse: melts on overcurrent (single-use). MCB: bimetal for overload + solenoid for short circuit (resettable, fast).

Part (b): Types of Earthing
Types of Earthing
1. PLATE EARTHING: Copper plate (60×60×3.18mm) or GI plate buried ≥3m deep. Surrounded by charcoal + salt layers → low resistance. Earth resistance: <1Ω (copper), <2Ω (GI). 2. PIPE EARTHING (Most Common in India): GI pipe (38mm dia, 2–3m long) driven vertically into moist soil. Funnel on top for water maintenance. Low cost, easy to install. 3. ROD EARTHING: Copper-clad steel rod (12–25mm dia, 1–3m) driven into ground. Used where soil is hard/rocky. Multiple rods in parallel for lower R. 4. STRIP EARTHING: Copper or GI strip buried horizontally in a trench (0.5m deep). Used around large buildings and substations. 5. SYSTEM EARTHING: TN system: Neutral point of transformer directly earthed. TT system: Separate earth electrodes at source and consumer. IT system: Isolated neutral (used in hospitals, no direct earth). Requirements for Good Earthing: Earth resistance < 1Ω; min conductor 16mm² copper or 25mm² GI. Earth continuity conductor must NOT be fused or switched. Test every 6 months.
✅
Solar Plant: PV Panels→MPPT→Inverter→Transformer→Grid. Earthing types: Plate, Pipe (most common), Rod, Strip, and System — all provide low-resistance path to earth to protect persons and equipment.
